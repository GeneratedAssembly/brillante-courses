Every lesson in this course was built from two openly licensed sources. This
page states exactly what they are, what you may do with the result, and the
rules every lesson follows when quoting or borrowing from them.

## The two sources

> **Barbastathis, George, Colin Sheppard, and Se Baek Oh.** *2.71 Optics*.
> Spring 2009. Massachusetts Institute of Technology: MIT OpenCourseWare,
> <https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-71-optics-spring-2009/>. License: Creative
> Commons BY-NC-SA.

> **Ling, Samuel J., Jeff Sanny, and William Moebs.** *University Physics
> Volume 3*. OpenStax, <https://openstax.org/details/books/university-physics-volume-3>.
> Freely accessed through LibreTexts,
> <https://phys.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/University_Physics/University_Physics_(OpenStax)/University_Physics_III_-_Optics_and_Modern_Physics_(OpenStax)>.
> License: Creative Commons BY 4.0.

MIT OCW 2.71/2.710 supplies the structure, the derivations, and the working
engineer's perspective: 26 sessions moving from ray tracing through
aberrations, wave optics, interferometry, and diffraction, up to imaging
systems and resolution. OpenStax *University Physics III* (chapters 1–4 only)
supplies the introductory-physics treatment of the same phenomena — the nature
of light, geometric optics, interference, and diffraction — which we use where
a gentler first pass helps.

These two licenses are compatible, but not symmetrically. Because MIT's terms
are stricter, the **course as a whole is released under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**: you
must give credit, you may not use it commercially, and any adaptation that
redistributes its content must carry the same license. Where a passage draws
*only* on OpenStax material, that passage may be reused under CC BY 4.0 alone.
Per-figure and per-quote captions state which license governs that item.

## Citation policy

Every lesson follows these rules, and this page is the contract:

1. **Paraphrase by default.** Lesson text is our own wording.
2. **Verbatim excerpts** appear only inside blockquotes with an explicit
   citation line and URL, as done for the two sources above.
3. **Borrowed figures** live in the course `images/` directory with a caption
   ending in the source, license, and URL — e.g.
   `(Source: MIT OCW 2.71 Optics, Spring 2009, lecture 15, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 — https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-71-optics-spring-2009/)` —
   or the OpenStax equivalent under CC BY 4.0.
4. **Downloadables always link out.** Lecture videos, notes PDFs, problem
   sets, and exams are never re-hosted here; they appear as link cards so
   traffic and credit flow to MIT. The same goes for the OpenStax/LibreTexts
   book.
5. **Every lesson ends with a Sources footer** listing what it drew on —
   MIT lecture numbers and OpenStax sections — with links to the canonical
   pages.
